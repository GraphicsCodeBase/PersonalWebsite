# Style Language — what does "me" look like?

> The room has to feel like *my* head, not a generic asset pack. Style is a decision I make **in 2D first**, then apply to everything. We'll refine this as we go.
> Nothing here is locked. This note is a conversation with myself.

---

## Step 1 — Pick a lane (choose ONE, everything follows)
| Direction | Feels like | Reference vibe | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cute low-poly** | Warm, approachable, toy-like | The freeCodeCamp room video, Animal Crossing | Easy |
| **Cosy realistic** | Actual lived-in bedroom, warm lamps | Studio apartment at night | Hard |
| **Retro / 90s tech den** | CRTs, beige plastic, cables, posters | Old bedroom + dial-up era | Medium |
| **Clean minimal / muji** | Calm, ordered, lots of empty space | Architectural renders | Medium |
| **Surreal brain-space** | Not a real room — floating shelves, impossible geometry | Literal "inside my head" | Medium |
| **Pixel / voxel** | Playful, nostalgic, forgiving | Minecraft-ish dioramas | Easy |

- **Shapes:** rounded and soft, or sharp and geometric?
- **Type pairing** (for the 2D overlay UI): heading / body

## Step 3 — Lock it with a reference sheet
Generate/collect **one image board** that represents the target. Every later decision gets checked against it: *"does this belong in that picture?"*

## Personality inputs (answer these, they feed the style)
These are the questions that actually make it *me* rather than a nice room:
- What do I do when I'm not coding?
- What's on my real walls?
- What's the messiest part of my brain? What's the most organised?
- What music is playing in this room?
- What would a friend say is unmistakably "me"?
- What's one thing I'd want a stranger to find if they poked around?

_(Answer them badly and quickly. Bad fast answers beat no answers.)_
